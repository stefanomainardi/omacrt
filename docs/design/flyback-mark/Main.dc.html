Flyback · the mark
The ramp, and the stroke back
A television's horizontal deflection is a sawtooth: the current ramps across the screen, then collapses back in almost no time at all. That collapse is the flyback, and the transformer named after it is the part that dies when the line rate is wrong. The mark is that waveform — four bars stepping out on the parent mark's 8 thick, 4 gap rhythm, and one full-height stroke where the ramp resets.

Everything is a whole unit. No hairline, no diagonal, no gradient: the mark has to survive being drawn into 240 scan lines by a beam whose spot is about a line wide, and a shape that changes when it shrinks is a patch rather than a system.

What changed since the sketch. The return stroke was 6 units wide; it is 8 now, the same weight as a bar, because it is the same beam — only travelling a hundred times faster. That also puts every edge of the mark on the 8/4 rhythm, so the whole thing is one weight and reads at 16 px.
